# Endless Arena

The arena provides repeated live fights for tuning combat before campaign missions. It uses a 112 × 80 m yard with four broad entrances, tall crates, low barriers, loose crates and clear flanking lanes. Outer cover breaks up the expanded space. The player starts with the chosen four-robot squad selected in a square, viewed through the wider isometric camera.

## Loadouts and orders

Assault robots retain machine guns and grenades. NEEDLE has the existing braced sniper rifle and a compact pistol, with no grenades or machine gun. The pistol deals 22 damage, reaches 28 m, holds 12 rounds, cycles every 0.35 s and reloads in 1.6 s. Its magazine, cycling and reload are independent of the rifle. Q chooses the available close weapon and cycles gun/pistol when both models are selected; E chooses the rifle; G requires a selected assault robot. Weapon buttons reflect available equipment. A mixed firearm order only fires equipped operators. An automatic order fires selected machine guns and miniguns together with their own magazines/cadences. The twin-minigun configuration equips ROOK (2) and SPINDLE (4) with independent 240-round belts; ANCHOR and LATCH retain machine guns and grenade rotation. The sniper configuration has three assault robots plus NEEDLE; BOLT adds a fourth grenadier in the machine-gun configuration. Specialists cannot throw grenades. The [squad selector and miniguns](minigun.md) are available in the arena.

The pistol uses the supplied `854226__qubodup__m4a1-rifle-shot-5.wav` without altering the recording. The primary weapon model, actual muzzle origin, muzzle flash, cover/range guide, magazine and sample all follow the selected firearm. Machine guns and miniguns default to 1.65 m aim over empty ground, clearing the arena's 1.25–1.3 m barriers. A live target's lower-body hover aims at its upper chest; higher cursor hits and elevated silhouettes retain their height. AI and support automatic gunners prefer upper-body sight lines, with a lower-body fallback if needed. Tall crates still require a flank. Ground movement, grenades and first-person rifle controls retain their existing behavior.

## Friendly fire and sniper support

Friendly fire is enabled for grenades and the sniper rifle on both teams. Rifle projectiles, optical aim rays and previews stop at allied hulls, including disabled ones; a living ally takes the same damage, suppression and impulse as an opponent. Grenades affect the thrower, allies and opponents according to distance and blast exposure. Machine gun, minigun and pistol rays still ignore allied hulls. World geometry blocks shots and blast pressure, and loose props retain their physical reactions. Only hostile hits contribute to manual accuracy and grenade-hit counts.

Entering first-person sniping puts every other living player robot on temporary cover duty, even when only NEEDLE is selected. Support does not change their stance or paths: pending movement and queued destinations continue while they shoot, with the ordinary firing movement speed and moving-shot spread. Idle robots remain in place. They prefer nearby visible hostiles, sample body and upper-body sight lines, acquire before short bursts, and reload their actual equipped firearms. Hits briefly stagger support, pausing walking and firing while preserving their orders and physical knockback. Bracing clears stagger faster. Recovery permits bursts between repeated hits; suppression does not keep extending their attack delay. They never chase enemies, attack practice targets or throw automatic grenades. Cover aim is independent of the player's aim and trigger; `coverShots` and `coverHits` are separate from manual accuracy.

Only the sniper operator and its indicators are hidden during the scope render pass. Teammates stay visible because they can intercept rifle shots. The scope shows the living cover count and squad cards show COVER. Ordinary scope exit stops automatic fire without changing supporting robots’ movement orders or stance. Weapon/selection changes, pause, reset, range changes, and NEEDLE's death also stop cover fire and clear its state. Casualties never rejoin support.

## Wave loop

- The first wave follows a four-second entrance warning and contains three assault robots.
- Each clear increases the next wave's size by one robot, capped at twelve: three squads of at most four. There is no last wave.
- Each squad enters through its own marked gate, with stable nearby rally slots and different flank preferences. Gates rotate between waves; warning plans prefer gates at least 12 m from survivors. A player moving into an announced entrance causes spawn slots to shift within that entrance.
- Waves 1–2 keep every enemy unbraced. From wave 3, enemies brace at visible firing positions and each full four-member squad includes a fragile sniper. Enemy snipers switch to their pistol at close range.
- Surviving players receive full integrity and ammunition when all hostiles and live explosives are gone. Casualties remain disabled. A six-second entrance warning gives time to regroup.
- Squad loss stops enemy attacks and the wave loop, releases held fire and scope mode, and shows a restart button. Shift+R restores the entire arena. Pausing stops simulation time and audio.

The wave HUD reports hostiles, cumulative hostile casualties and surviving players. Enemy fire and enemy grenades are tracked separately from player shooting accuracy and grenade statistics. No progression, economy, spawn-cost budget or missions are introduced.

## Enemy combat

Enemies walk through the same finite-acceleration controller and use the same capsule colliders, route finding, local hull avoidance, magazines, reloads, muzzle rays, damage and impulses as the player. Moving robot hulls never become static route-planning obstacles. Displacement beyond an open entrance permits inward recovery rather than a permanent boundary stall.

Enemy intentions update every 0.15 s. They prefer nearby living targets, with a small preference for the current target to avoid rapid switching. They check actual sight lines to the body and upper body; tall obstruction causes them to seek a different firing position and flank it. Route requests are capped to one per 1.2 s per advancing enemy. Enemies use their own aim points without changing the player's aim or scope.

Assault units acquire before firing short three-to-five-shot bursts, with pauses that shorten over the early waves. They engage within 38 m and seek firing positions around 30 m away, leaving room for pressure and flanking. Waves 1–2 stop to shoot without bracing; later waves brace when they acquire a clear firing position. Hits pause locomotion and attacks, but retain external momentum and pending movement orders. Recovery uses the same braced/unbraced rates as the player. Automatic bursts can resume while a robot is still sliding between staggers; rifle operators require stable bracing before their slower shots. Entry acquisition can begin after 2.5 s once stagger clears, even if pressure keeps a robot away from its rally slot. Orange rings pulse during acquisition, stagger brightens a ring and jolts the torso, and live hostile health bars distinguish the combatants from practice plates. See [kinetic combat tuning](combat-feel.md).

From wave 4, assault units can lob a grenade at two or more players clustered within 4 m, at a distance of 10–24 m, provided allies are at least 8 m away. The same four-second grenade cooldown applies, with an eight-second AI planning delay. Only one hostile grenade is live at once. An incoming message and pulsing orange ring under its current position warn of its possible blast radius; this is not a promise of an unobstructed blast. Actual ballistic flight, bounces, the 2.4 s fuse and cover samples determine the result. NEEDLE cannot throw on either team.

## Lifetime and verification

Dead hostile bodies persist briefly, then retire after eight seconds or before the next wave. Rendering removes their model, health bar, ring, flash and owned GPU buffers. Shared geometry/materials remain reusable. Live-wave population caps at twelve, and transient effects retain their existing fixed budgets.

The simulation test suite uses the actual Rapier simulation. Added checks cover restricted loadouts, mixed weapon cycling, independent pistol reload and reach, warm-up and physical squad entry, actual enemy damage and independent aim, cover-clipped fire, survivor refits and permanent casualties, enemy snipers, hostile grenades and separate statistics, waiting for live explosives, occupied entrance slots, fourteen clears into wave fifteen with bounded body counts, defeat/reset, and recovering through an open boundary. Further checks exercise both teams’ weapon-specific friendly fire, grenade damage and impulses to the owner and allies, unselected support executing queued move orders while firing, independent aim and accuracy, blocked target acquisition, suppression/reloading, casualty exclusion, scope exits and a teammate crossing the optical sight line. Existing drills still pass using assault robots for explosives. Browser validation checks contextual weapon controls, decoded pistol audio and native pistol fire, scope entry during pistol reload, arena bursts, player firing and wave clear, subsequent entry, pause, restart, and desktop/narrow framing. The support flow also checks native machine-gun/pistol immunity and friendly grenade damage, a scoped rifle hit on a visible teammate, cover bursts with idle LMB, steady scope orientation, queued movement continuing during sniping and after RMB exit, scope transitions, and cover indicators at desktop, laptop and narrow sizes. Captured aiming and aimed rifle shots use DOM mouse events to avoid headless cursor-recentering warps; keyboard capture, overhead fire, grenades and movement use native input.

This is a starting balance for playtesting. Software-GPU Chromium checks establish functional behavior, not hardware frame rate or mix quality. Firefox/Safari and physical mouse/audio-device checks remain open.
