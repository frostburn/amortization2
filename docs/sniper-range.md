# NEEDLE and Long Range

Robot 4 is NEEDLE: a pale, narrow chassis with exposed joints, a long barrel, an optic, and a folding bipod. It trades durability and mass for a rifle that can disable an exposed practice target in one shot. The other three models remain assault robots. NEEDLE carries a rifle and pistol, with no machine gun or grenades. The three assault robots retain machine guns and grenades. All four share the existing selection, formation, steering, and queued-movement controls.

## Combat tuning

| Integrity                      | 160          | 64           |
| Mass                           | 90 kg        | 48 kg        |
| Gun damage against targets     | 6            | 140          |
| Gun reach                      | 65 m         | 140 m        |
| Magazine                       | 90           | 5            |
| Shot interval                  | 0.07145625 s | 1.4 s        |
| Reload                         | 2.2 s        | 3 s          |
| Self-recoil, unbraced / braced | 5 / 1 N·s    | 180 / 12 N·s |

Selecting a single robot chooses an available firearm; selecting NEEDLE leaves grenade mode. Q selects its pistol or an assault machine gun, and cycles between those arms in a mixed selection. E selects the rifle, and G selects grenades only when an assault robot is selected. Switching weapons preserves the rifle's sight but exits first-person sniping. A mixed selection fires only the robots equipped for the chosen weapon. A rifle shot is a ray through the same physics world as machine-gun shots: solid cover stops it, impacts transfer momentum, and disabled targets topple. Target colliders use the same local shape and facing as their meshes. On disable, the current facing and interpolation pose pass to physics before rotations unlock, so impacts tip the target away without a sideways orientation snap.

Space toggles braced first-person sniping when the rifle is selected. NEEDLE braces and the other living squad members provide automatic cover fire, regardless of selection. Their existing movement and queued destinations continue; support does not force them to brace or change their orders. Ordinary scope exit stops automatic fire without changing their stance or movement. Support aims at visible hostile robots independently of the scope, uses real magazines and reloads, reacts to suppression and stops on scope exit. It never attacks practice plates. The scope readout counts covering robots and their cards show COVER. Key release and keyboard repeat do not change the mode. NEEDLE needs 0.6 seconds of slow, stable footing before its rifle settles to a 0.0006 rad sway envelope. An unbraced rifle starts with a 0.04 rad envelope, amplified by recoil and instability. Deterministic rifle sway changes the shot around the sight line. The etched scope crosshair stays fixed at the optical centre; settling and reload state are conveyed by its colour and the readout. Unbraced self-recoil reduces stability by 0.55 and delivers actual backward momentum. The finite motor acceleration recovers from that movement. Moving, selection changes, weapon changes, pause, death, reset, and range changes exit sniping and release its support. The machine gun and pistol use hold-to-brace controls.

NEEDLE’s pistol has 12 rounds, 22 damage, 28 m reach, a 0.35 s interval, a 1.6 s reload, and substantially shorter barrel reach. Its ammunition, reload and cycling state are independent of the five-round rifle. The visible gun and muzzle flash switch to a compact pistol model; the supplied soft M4A1 single shot (`854226`) serves as its sound. It never uses the machine-gun loop. NEEDLE is excluded from grenade eligibility, cooldown calculations and round-robin throw orders.

## Horizontal lane

The separate range spans x = −18…126 m and z = −16…16 m, with a wide isometric overview and extra space around the firing lane. NEEDLE starts at x = −2 m; targets stand at x = 28, 58, and 88 m, staggered across the lane so nearer plates do not hide the distant plate. These are nominal 30 / 60 / 90 m marks. The first target stands on the ground; the second and third stand on 2 m and 5 m concrete platforms. Their spawn heights and support colliders agree with the rendered decks. Platforms participate in navigation and stop low shots; live targets remain supported under gravity. The barrel pitches toward the aim, and the muzzle and shot use the full three-dimensional direction. Low side cover, a crate, the firing line, distance boards, and a backstop give scale and obstruction examples. No hostile AI is introduced.

The overview fits the full lane across the desktop viewport. The scene, collision geometry, destination placement, and navigation grid all use the chosen range's bounds. Switching ranges resets the drill, supplies, held input, and explosives, rebuilds the environment, and restores its overview. The original three drills remain available in Proving Ground. Only rifle kills complete the new three-target drill; a wrong-weapon kill requires a reset.

## First-person sniping

Space enters the first-person view at any overhead zoom. A perspective camera at the robot's eye height renders the real scene across the entire field, using the existing renderer and shadow map. Only the operator and its indicators are hidden during this pass. Allied robots remain visible because rifle shots and optical rays can hit their hulls. The operator’s ordinary visibility is restored immediately after this render. An inexpensive transparent overlay adds a fixed etched reticle and a circular scope vignette; there is no auxiliary render target or second world pass. The bottom readout shows sight distance, relative height, settling and reload state without covering a target at the top of the view.

Sight buttons choose a distant target without firing. They preserve the wide overview. In sniping, Space requests mouse capture. Relative mouse motion turns the camera with yaw and pitch, limited short of vertical, while the centre ray aims through the real scene. Turning continues beyond screen edges without holding a drag button. Orientation is independent of the aim point, so a stationary mouse cannot create view drift. Invert X axis and Invert Y axis independently reverse mouse motion and are saved with the existing settings. If mouse capture is unavailable, ordinary mouse movement still turns the view and a stationary pointer near an edge continues turning. The same inversion settings apply to that fallback. Clicking retries capture. Aim rays use the physics world and never snap to a target's centre or height. The wheel adjusts the scope from 6 to 24 degrees of vertical field of view; default is 12 degrees. The reticle is part of the scope overlay, fixed at its optical centre. It never follows a target intersection, muzzle/eye parallax, recoil or spread, and turns mint after settling. Space, right click, or Escape leaves sniping, restoring the exact overhead camera. A second Escape pauses. Leaving sniping releases mouse capture; capture loss, focus loss and gameplay transitions also clear held fire. The header controls become reachable again on return, and the common overhead crosshair remains available at all zoom levels.

## Rifle sound

The close recording is `855602`, the field recording `855606`, and reload uses `855601`. Camera distance drives an equal-power crossfade: close only through 24 m, a blend between 24 and 64 m, and distant only beyond 64 m. Gain falls gently with distance. Stereo placement uses the shooter's position relative to the active camera, rather than a fixed origin in the yard. The orthographic camera's physical offset scales with zoom; first-person sniping uses the eye as the listening position. The two attacks are aligned to within a millisecond by a 5 ms playback offset on the close recording; both tails remain intact. Rifle shots never start the machine-gun loop. The source files and CC0 manifest remain unchanged.

## Verification

The suite contains 65 tests against the real physics simulation and perspective camera. Sniper checks cover model fragility and weapon eligibility, hits and drill completion at all distances and elevations, recoil and an unbraced miss, settling and movement release, firing cadence and reload, cover and attribution, far-lane movement, camera-relative audio, sniping transitions, and supported targets with height-sensitive shots. New regressions check facing continuity at disable, impact-directed toppling, a robot that turns before falling, all four axis-inversion combinations against the actual projected scene, stationary centre rays, pitch limits, and an elevated centre-ray rifle kill. Two further checks verify pitched machine-gun muzzle origins, elevated hits, cover-clipped aim guides and the shared 65 m range for previews and tracers. The original 21 range tests continue to pass with assault robots handling grenade drills. A further perspective-camera check puts a teammate across the scope and verifies that both the centre ray and actual rifle shot stop at the ally, transferring full damage without moving the fixed scope orientation or counting a friendly hit toward accuracy. Pistol and arena checks are documented in [Arena combat](arena.md).

Production Chromium 153 with a software GPU was checked at 1672 × 941, 1366 × 768, and 640 × 900. The browser flow covers Space capture, steady aiming, both-axis turning beyond the screen width, a low platform shot, 30/60/90 m kills, scope zoom, reload, native mouse-button firing, inversion combinations and page-reload persistence, Space/Escape/right-click exits, weapon and robot changes, fallback mouse aiming and continuous edge turning, range switching, and square squad movement. Captured turning and aimed shots use DOM relative mouse events because headless Chromium's virtual cursor produces recentering warps for absolute automation input; real keyboard capture and native mouse buttons are checked separately. The fallback uses ordinary native mouse movement. The Browser plugin was unavailable, so these checks used Playwright. Hardware mouse delivery, GPU performance, speaker/headphone balance, and Firefox/Safari remain untested.

The latest aim-feedback check uses the actual machine-gun model and native hover/fire controls beside the 2 m platform: the barrel pitches up, the target marker sits at its aim height, a stem marks the ground projection, a cover-clipped guide shows the nominal shot line, and the hint reports relative height before any shots. Level aim, mixed squad selection and grenade previews also work. Pixel checks compare the scope crosshair centre across 30/60/90 m targets, view movement, zoom, a rifle shot and desktop/narrow viewports.
