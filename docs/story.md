# Story outline — Futures Contract

Campaign proposal for **2038**, twelve years after the first game's events. The
original campaign did not establish a calendar year; this is the sequel's working
chronology. These notes guide future missions and scenes. The playable build includes four tutorial contracts, two armed operations,
and the combat, district and civilian prototypes.

## Continuity

The original crew won. The repayments remain paid, the seizure orders remain
withdrawn, and the liberated district retains local control of its infrastructure.
People are free to leave or stay. The crew's homecoming with Iona Voss and Ren
Quill happened, and all six return here. The sequel does not require a particular
arrest or lethal outcome for Holt or Kestrel, or bring back a defeated antagonist
as a secret superior.

Morrow, Vale, Rook and Sable have moved from direct armed intervention into
operations and support for a robot squad. Their original responsibilities remain
recognizable: field lead, systems, security and reconnaissance. See
[the friendly cast](friendly-cast.md) for their individual roles and portraits.

## The world

The liberated district is doing well. Its workshops, independent businesses and
local utilities have survived long enough to become an example that neighbouring
communities want to follow. That success creates the sequel's conflict.

The surrounding city depends on overlapping private service agreements. One
company runs transport, another water, another freight. Municipal institutions
still exist, but own little equipment and cannot instantly resolve an armed
dispute. Companies trade claims on future district revenue. Agreements include
step-in rights allowing equipment, premises and operating rights to pass to a
new operator when the existing service fails. These are fictional arrangements
for this setting, rather than a description of contemporary financial contracts.

Deliberately interrupting a competitor's service can make its future cheap to
buy. Security robots, recovery contractors and emergency administrators fight
over that opportunity while each describes its actions as restoring continuity.
Witnesses, ownership and legitimate public authority still matter; force lets
a company establish facts on the ground before those facts can be challenged.

The streets remain familiar: apartment balconies, repair shops, supermarkets,
school crossings, loading bays and a waterfront promenade. CART delivers food,
CRATE visits a parcel cabinet, KITE follows its flight corridor, and PORTER
unloads a washing machine. Cheap fleets coexist with premium machines. Their
engineering vocabulary follows [the civilian roster](civilian-robots.md).
Combat interrupts ordinary work. Local people should have reasons to return.

## Why the squad is called

The crew has established an independent response outfit: a garage, a transport
vehicle, four expensive combat machines and more work than expected. Voss helped
develop and harden the platforms. The outfit owns its equipment and maintains
it independently, including removing manufacturer-controlled ownership locks.
Exceptional squad capability has physical limits: weight, recoil, cooling,
maintenance and demanding duty cycles.

Neighbourhood associations, independent utilities and small businesses hire
the crew when normal assistance cannot reach them or their existing provider
has been instructed to wait. Early contracts concern concrete emergencies:
recover seized cargo, clear a depot, escort a repair convoy, keep an evacuation
route open or protect a service restarting under pressure.

The robots supply onboard movement and combat autonomy. The player issues
squad orders and takes direct aiming control. The human team supplies local
knowledge, preparation, judgement and responsibility. They travel with the
operation and arrange recovery; they remain people with their own jobs rather
than uploaded minds inside the chassis.

Payment keeps the workshop operating. Successful contracts build relationships
and assets that let the outfit compete with the companies causing the trouble.
Clients can be mistaken or self-interested; Morrow decides what the crew accepts.

## Opposing interests

| Party | What it wants | How the player encounters it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gannet Logistics | Acquire independent freight and loading operations through interruptions and disputed recoveries. | Seized containers, loading equipment removals, guarded depots and heavy recovery squads. |
| Meridian Services | Control transport and neighbourhood service networks, including competitors' access to them. | Traffic corridors, service yards, useful offers against Gannet and contracts that expose Meridian's own leverage. |
| Civic Guarantee | Become the sole operator capable of guaranteeing the city's essential services. | Emergency financing and recovery assistance, followed by accumulated takeover rights and coordinated enforcement. |

**Mara Senn**, Civic Guarantee's director, appears early as a composed, useful
person. Her guarantees make repairs possible after several operations. She
believes competing providers have demonstrated that they cannot govern essential
infrastructure. She finances competing interests, profits from failures and
accumulates the authority and assets to replace them. She manufactures some
of the evidence for the conclusion she already intends to reach.

Her ambition is concrete. She wants an operating city under a single authority,
with herself deciding which needs have priority. She can defend that position
intelligently and commit ruthless acts in its service.

## Campaign progression

The five movements below establish causal order. They do not fix the mission
count. Each can support several small districts and combat-focused operations.

### 1. Receiving

The [first playable contract](first-mission.md) starts with a smaller dispute:
release a cooperative's door motors and kettle elements from a pickup yard.
Four hired pistol guards enforce a storage hold. Morrow accepts a limited
assignment, Vale knows the dispatcher, and reopening access gets idle PORTERs
moving again. The wider seizures below develop after this modest intervention.

A freight cooperative at the marine port hires the crew to recover impounded
containers. Gannet's security then begins removing its loading equipment as well.
The crew protects workers, recovers the cargo and gets the depot moving.

Suggested operations: reclaim a loading yard; escort departing service vans past
a recovery squad. The opening image is a PORTER still holding a box beside a
locked stand: its work has become impossible, but nobody cancelled the task.

Rook and Sable introduce the squad at the range. Morrow introduces the contract;
Vale supplies the local picture. Voss examines the first damaged machine after
its return. Quill discovers that Gannet bought future operating rights shortly
before the cooperative's business was interrupted.

The playable tutorial sequence continues through [Crossing](crossing.md) and
[Handling](handling.md), then [Release](escort.md). Quill visits Gannet's records
office himself to inspect those operating rights. Its hired security locks him
in for "account reconciliation"; the robot team breaks in through reception. Quill opens the rear staff exit,
and the team escorts him along the back alley while covering an arriving
response squad on the eastern street. His second rescue brings a person into the field
and returns him, with the records, to his work on the cooperative's agreements.

[Priority Access](priority-access.md) is the first armed operation after Release.
Gannet's contractor occupies the independent neighbourhood exchange and prepares
to remove dispatch equipment. The crew stops removal, reopens physical access
and covers the owners' technicians against a street-and-concourse response.
Deliveries resume in the district. Meridian then offers priority network access
in exchange for control of dispatch, introducing its useful but conditional help.

### 2. Right of Way

[Recovery Fee](recovery-fee.md) follows Priority Access. Gannet is moving the
cooperative's seized charging racks and drive assemblies out of the district.
The squad intercepts the convoy, disables its cargo truck and fights a finite
security detail with two machinegunners. Its dispatch case must be carried
back under pursuit from a small Gannet patrol while the cooperative collects
the bulk equipment. Securing an intact load restores more
of the cooperative's capacity; destroying it leaves a salvage recovery. Quill
finds the equipment was sold before seizure was authorised, with an extra
payment for delivery that day. A buyer beyond the local contractor now has a
reason to take an interest in the squad.

Meridian offers help against Gannet and submits requests of its own. The crew
fights through streets, service yards and utility approaches. Some assignments
restore useful service; others would hand a choke point to another company.
Morrow begins refusing those terms and building relationships with independent
operators. Civic Guarantee's assistance makes it a recurring presence.

Suggested operations: protect a repair convoy; clear a transit approach while
keeping a departing civilian convoy out of the crossfire. District victories
have visible consequences: deliveries resume, a crossing opens, technicians
arrive or a workshop becomes a later recovery point.

### 3. Quiet Enjoyment

Vale notices the same recovery vehicles arriving unusually quickly after
unrelated incidents. Sable follows the orders and records. Civic Guarantee has
been financing both competing providers and acquiring takeover rights whenever
their disputes interrupt service. Its guarantees also constrain the recipients.

Suggested operations: protect witnesses and recovered dispatch equipment;
hold a newly reopened service yard against a second seizure attempt. Evidence
is recovered through the fighting and explained in scenes, rather than requiring
slow terminal puzzles. Accurate footage alone is insufficient: Sable and Quill
establish context and responsibility with the people involved.

### 4. Margin Call

Independent operators combine their agreements, equipment and mutual guarantees.
The crew's outfit becomes capable of keeping a district functioning without
Civic Guarantee. Senn offers to acquire it. When the offer fails, she invokes
emergency authority and dispatches recovery squads.

Suggested operations: break a staging-yard offensive; move a service convoy
through contested blocks; prevent equipment seizures at several connected
approaches. The old liberated district supplies resources and volunteers.
Its first-game victory has become strong enough to help its neighbours.

The human team's work converges. Vale organises local contacts, Sable maintains
trustworthy communications, Rook prepares the machines and withdrawal routes,
Morrow coordinates the response, and Voss and Quill make its practical future
possible. Morrow must increasingly trust decisions made without her permission.

### 5. Futures Contract

Senn launches a coordinated takeover before the independent network begins
operating. Several small districts become pressure points. The player tackles
a sequence of decisive interventions rather than defending a city-sized map.

The final operation stops Senn's armoured mobile command vehicle at a major
interchange. She is personally present to authorise disputed seizures, coordinate
contractors who distrust each other and demonstrate her ability to restore order.
Her organised recovery division is the boss encounter: escorts, reserve squads,
changing firing posts and command equipment that can be exposed and disabled.
Senn herself has ordinary human vulnerability.

Miniguns herd advancing formations; machinegunners hold approaches; NEEDLE
eliminates exposed equipment and elevated specialists; grenades punish enemies
forced into poor positions. Defeating command capability progressively breaks
the organised offensive. Enemy machines retain their own local autonomy.
Physical control of the interchange lets allied service crews enter and secure
the assets being taken. The outcome is an operational defeat with witnesses and
consequences, rather than a universal remote shutdown.

## Ending

The independent coalition acquires the equipment and operating rights used
against it. The player comes out on top with workshops, depots, clients, employees
and enough force to make its agreements stick. Morrow now holds some of the
power the crew spent two games confronting. The ending shows her exercising it:
communities can terminate their contracts, own their equipment and obtain
protection without surrendering their future.

The last scene is an ordinary afternoon at the garage. Deliveries move outside.
Someone brought lunch. Rook starts lifting a replacement assembly onto the bench;
a PORTER reaches for it. He hesitates, then lets go.

> ROOK: All right. You can have that job.

The combat machines cool behind them, weapons safe. The humans have time to eat.
End on the victory and the life it enables.

## Scene and mission discipline

- Briefings explain the immediate objective and relevant circumstances. Optional
  scenes provide relationships and wider context; skipping them preserves mission
  comprehension.
- Most operations have one briefing owner and one principal live contact. A new
  speaker signals a meaningful change. Do not narrate routine visible actions.
- Reports have identifiable sources and limits. Vale does not see through every
  roof; Sable cannot substitute a network command for a physical operation.
- Routine weapon readiness, damage and positioning remain visual feedback.
  Dialogue introduces behaviours once or reports exceptions that matter.
- Contracts, claims and evidence motivate concrete fights. The sequel's emphasis
  remains fast decisions, physical pressure, squad movement and purposeful aiming.
- Recurring civilian activity, accessible buildings and returning contacts make
  a compact district feel like part of a larger city. Completed objectives alter
  later access, service or relationships rather than merely changing a label.
- Supporting humans work near the operation. Their danger is consequential when
  it occurs, but they do not become a recurring sequence of helpless captives.
